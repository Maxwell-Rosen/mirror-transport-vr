# Setting

The entire game takes place inside a simple, low-poly museum. Although the museum is simple, it is quite beautiful, elegant, and pleasing to be inside. The walls are a neutral grey-beige and extend up to a tall ceiling. The museum features fluted, warm limestone octagonal columns with square bases and circular molding above each base. A beak molding runs along the foot of the walls. The floor is matte stone, a shade darker than the walls so the edge of the room is easy to see. Low wooden benches between the columns give the rooms a human scale.

## Entry foyer

After choosing **Enter the museum** on the title screen, visitors stand in the middle of the ribbed doormat just inside the glass entrance doors. The foyer is 8 m deep. Straight ahead, a wide arch opens onto the atrium and its fountain. Beside the opening, a short welcome panel reads “Welcome to Maxwell Rosen's PhD thesis. Explore how magnetic mirrors confine plasma through simulations.” A graphic on the other side shows the controls for the device in use. Above the opening, the arch reads “Mirror Transport” and “Museum of Plasma Physics.” Its sign, the two foyer panels and all other museum boards carry white lettering on matte black, including the title and pause menus. Both foyer panels sit low enough to read from a chair. Returning to the beginning brings the visitor back to the mat. [Controls](CONTROLS.md)

The oak ticket counter stands off to the left, with its **Tickets** plaque facing arrivals. An empty chair is tucked behind the writing pad, beside the ticket roll and a lit desk lamp. A small stack of folded museum brochures and one loose copy lie flat on the public side of the counter. Maxwell Rosen’s portrait in front of the copper coils hangs halfway along the wall to the left of the ticket desk. Across the aisle, photographs of a Princeton courtyard in bloom and a tower in evening light hang above the bench. Through the glass entrance doors, visitors can see a tree-lined garden path.

## Main atrium

The atrium is a long hall with eight exhibit spaces, four on each side. Skylights on both slopes of the pointed roof cast broad patches of daylight across the floor. The lighting in the atrium is inviting players into the exhibits, while also conveying a timeless presense. The first pair of columns frames a view down the hall to a spring fountain in the center of the atrium. Water gushes from a stone cleft, falls over planted ledges and ripples across a broad basin. Plants and benches sit along the sides. From a bench by the animated fountain, the player can look back along the row of entrances and choose where to go next.

Look back at the foyer arch from the fountain to see Ammar Hakim and Greg Hammett above the opening, facing down the hall. Walking away from the foyer, visitors pass each numbered sign, its arch, then one photograph. On a wall shared by two entrances, the photograph hangs near one arch and the next gallery’s sign hugs the other. This leaves the short walls beside the first two signs open. The frames and signs are 1.40 m tall and share a centre height of 1.74 m. The foyer and atrium hold the personal photographs so visitors can spend time with them between exhibits.

For the first layout, try an atrium 16 m wide with gallery openings 4 m wide. Keep a level, clear path at least 3 m wide past the fountain on either side. The space should feel quite large. Arrange the wall between entrances in thirds. Centre the column and put a low bench directly in front of the gallery sign on one side. Set the planter toward the column to give visitors a clear view of the photograph and its caption. A bench sits beneath the Sinfonia cello section opposite the fountain. Leave a visible margin of wall between each sign and its doorframe. Check these provisional dimensions from both seated and standing height in the headset.

## Exhibit entrances

Each doorway has a large exhibit number on the atrium wall, visible as the player walks down the hall. While an exhibit is under development, yellow-and-black striped tape stretches from jamb to jamb across its entrance at waist height. The sign beside the number reads **Under construction**.

When an exhibit opens, the tape comes down and the exhibit title goes beside its number. The visitor can read the exhibit question on the main display through the opening. The return view through each opening includes the skylit hall. Inside the galleries, light falls evenly across the apparatus and its labels. Keep the wall behind each plot plain so that the colours of the data are easy to distinguish.

From the foyer, the first left entrance leads to **1 · The Magnetic Mirror**. Its wide floor stripe starts the exhibit as the visitor crosses into the room. The first right entrance opens into **2 · Between Bounces**. Its sculptures occupy the original 16 m depth. Visitors pass the pendulums, dye river and whirlpool on their right before reaching the inclined bead rings. [Mirror room and sightlines](gallery-1/README.md#room-and-sightlines) · [Between Bounces floor plan](gallery-2/exhibit-2-POA.md#the-walk-from-the-atrium)

The second opening on the left leads to **3 · Time Dilation**. The 34 m room follows the right wall from the wave rings to the time-dilation mask and its two colour bars. Its print faces it across the aisle. After 3.7 m of clear floor, one tabletop relief shows permitted time step as height over axial position and parallel velocity. Its front edge selects five magnetic moments and switches time dilation off or on. Farther along, two framed volumes compare the uncapped and saved time limits at the same threshold.
