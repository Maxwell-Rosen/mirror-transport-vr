# Museum music

Aim for gentle, elegant chamber music that leaves room for a visitor to read an exhibit label or listen to the fountain. The opening clarinet in Mussorgsky’s first *Promenade* sets the tone for the museum. It has the warmth of someone singing quietly and a slightly somber color.

## Title quartet

*Threshold* lasts 30 seconds. The clarinet takes up the Promenade’s opening G–F over a bowed B-flat, then passes the rising answer to the cello. Harp arpeggios and soft piano chords carry two pairs of 5/4 and 6/4 bars at 44 bpm. The last chord rings into the opening of the next loop. As the title fades to black, let the quartet taper to silence. Bring the opening cello in as the foyer comes into view. During a pause, carry the Promenade through at a gentler level, then ease it back up on resume. The note score and sample renderer are in the project. ## The Promenade

Keep the quarter note at **40 bpm throughout**, including the middle variation. Shape the line with a small swell into a sustained note, a taper at the end of a phrase and space for a breath. The quicker notes fit inside the same slow pulse.

- **Bowed cellos** open with a single low B-flat. Give it two beats to swell. Three seconds in, the clarinet enters on G–F while the bow is still sounding. Overlap quiet bow changes so the opening ambience carries under the tune and through the player’s breaths.
- **The clarinet** carries the opening and returns after the harp’s middle verse. Use the soft sustained take in its lower register. Give the first note of each phrase a gentle attack and connect the notes that follow. Listen to the top of each phrase for the same rounded timbre. A bright, hard-edged note can sound harsh even at low volume.
- **The concert harp** plays quiet chord tones on beats one and four beneath the clarinet. Let them ring together. When two strings sound as a chord, pluck them on the same beat. In bars 9–11 the harp takes the melody for a whole phrase over the bowed bass. Give its longer notes room to decay. The clarinet and piano rest until the verse ends.
- **The grand piano** enters after the clarinet’s first statement, about 20 seconds in. Place an occasional soft two-note chord and let it ring.
