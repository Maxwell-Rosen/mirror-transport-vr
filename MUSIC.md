# Museum music

Aim for gentle, elegant chamber music that leaves room for a visitor to read an exhibit label or listen to the fountain. The opening clarinet in Mussorgsky’s first *Promenade* sets the tone for the museum. It has the warmth of someone singing quietly and a slightly somber color.

## The Promenade

Keep the quarter note at **40 bpm throughout**, including the middle variation. Shape the line with a small swell into a sustained note, a taper at the end of a phrase and space for a breath. The quicker notes fit inside the same slow pulse.

- **Bowed cellos** open with a single low B-flat. Give it two beats to swell. Three seconds in, the clarinet enters on G–F while the bow is still sounding. Overlap quiet bow changes so the opening ambience carries under the tune and through the player’s breaths.
- **One clarinet** carries all 24 bars, including the answer to the opening phrase. Use the soft sustained take in its lower register. Give the first note of each phrase a gentle attack and connect the notes that follow. Listen to the top of each phrase for the same rounded timbre. A bright, hard-edged note can sound harsh even at low volume.
- **The grand piano** enters after the clarinet’s first statement, about 20 seconds in. Place an occasional soft two-note chord and let it ring. Earlier arrangements felt crowded when the cello took over the tune and piano figures or harp echoes filled the pauses.

## Listening back

Compare revisions at the same playback level. Listen for the low bow beneath the clarinet’s entrance, tap the pulse through the middle variation around 1:35, and follow the final B-flat as its room decay meets the opening bow on the next lap.
